Think “Twenty Questions” 
Years ago, there was a television program named, “Guess My Line.”  People would appear hooded and the panel would, through a series of questions that could only be answered “Yes” or “No” within a set number of questions, guess their profession or identity.  
Here, we use a variation on that technique to capture the depth of organic knowledge of an expert in a given field of endeavor, then integrate that depth of knowledge with the breadth of artificial intelligence. 
Questions as Induction
Questions effect the mind differently than statements.  As I present this information, you seek in your mind, what we call “referential index.”  You seek an internal mental context to which you can attach incoming information so that it will be available for retrieval.  By this mostly subliminal methodology, people integrate new knowledge into their overall knowledge base.  The process of listening and learning is consciously passive while subliminally proactive.  You intake information, then proactively attach that information to a scaffold of referential index so that you can go back either in active consciousness or through a passive, internal reference process.  This internal scaffolding is the aspect of the mind we will attempt to access in direct conscious awareness by the artful use of questions.  
Unlike the standard lecture methods of presenting information, we use the unique quality of questions as they act on the mind much differently than statements. Lecturers will often attempt a mostly clumsy method of asking a question that the listener cannot easily answer.  This tends to grab the conscious mind of the listener and forces them toward what we call a “trans-dirivational index” search.  That is where they attempt to consciously access their mostly internal mental library, their scaffolding of referential index. 
Techniques of this nature, while effective toward accessing conscious attention, the effect this technique creates an internal pattern interruption.  The listener is knocked out of mental flow.  The internal mental flow of accessing and organizing new information into referential index is disrupted.  The modeling scaffold a person constructs to store new information in a way that makes it readily available later is interrupted at a level that is mostly out of conscious awareness.  
Pattern interruption is an extremely powerful tool for affecting the focus of attention and while a lecturer may keep their students awake and attentive, this has the effect of preventing the listener from effectively storing the information they are receiving, as the aspect that would be organizing the referential index is distracted by trying to anticipate interruptions.  
Directing the Inner Mind
We use questions as a way to direct mental flow rather than interrupt it. By asking questions a listener can easily answer, with a “Yes” or “No,” we direct the inner thinking process rather than interrupt it.  A question that, by its structure, does not allow for explanation, allows the respondent to respond without dropping out of mental flow so long as our question is congruent with the current mental context. 
Rapport 
By asking questions a respondent can easily answer with a simple “Yes” or “No” we direct the inner mind and create mental flow as follows: 
	“Were you arrested?”
	“Yes” 
	“Was first contact with the arresting officer based on a traffic stop.” 
By asking questions a respondent can easily answer with a single word responce, we can direct the conscious attention and focus their attention to specific issues then ask something like this: 
	“After arrest, were you taken directly to the nearest magistrate?” 
We know that they were not as that is not the common practice of police.  However, it is a statutory requirement.  We could tell the person what the police are supposed to do but that is not nearly as effective as asking a question about what they were supposed to do and, by so doing, create a question in the mind of the respondent so that we can then immediately answer that question, such as, when we ask if they were taken directly to the nearest magistrate, we already know the answer and we know the question we just created in their mind, “What is that about?”  Then we immediately use a popup in the questionnaire that gives extra information. When we create a question in their mind and a note pops up, they will tend to read it in order to get their internal question answered.  In this way, we give the respondent the impression that we are mentally on the same track and by that method, create rapport we can then use to lead the user to new knowledge and understanding. 

Extract Expertise
With the above in mind, we start by interviewing a professional in a given field of endeavor and have that professional start with no knowledge of a prospect's situation, then, by a series of yes/no questions, extract the current condition of a potential client or person in need of professional services.  Where possible, we ask questions that can only be answered with a yes, no, or that asks for a specific piece of information.  We avoid asking open-ended questions as people, especially concerning stressful situations, when given the opportunity to address an unstructured issue, tend to react and respond to emotional influences and stray from the specific to the general.  
The entire methodology is a bit complex for this presentation but, suffice it to say, using a recursive technique, we can extract the entire knowledge base of the professional and present it to a novice in the form of an interactive questionnaire.  
Yes/No Not Mabe So
We ask yes/no questions or propose a select list.  When an answer, although specific, can be misinterpreted, we provide a separate input box where the person can explain within the specific confines of the instant context, or when answers lead to contradictory or confusing results, we ask for an explanation limited to the scope of the currently defined issue.

Prospecting Tool
The first tool we are creating is a prospecting tool.  The questionnaires included were designed primarily for someone who has been arrested and is in need of a lawyer.  The questionnaires will be made available to the court when the court is asked to appoint counsel for an indigent defendant.  The court will ask the person to complete the questionnaire so that the tool can produce an analysis of the case in order to ease the burden on underpaid court-appointed counsel of the public defender.  There is an underlying purpose and that is to demonstrate to the defendant that they have legal remedy and that the court-appointed counsel or public defender does not have the time or resources to adequately seek that remedy and they would be best served by paid counsel.  After the user finishes the questionnaire, we will offer to send the output report to a number of lawyers so that user can get differing opinions concerning their potential claims and methods of staying out of jail.  We then send the report to a number of lawyers who have signed up to receive our solicitations for representation.  This will act as a proof of concept we will then take to the state bar and offer the project to the bar association as an added benefit to their members.
